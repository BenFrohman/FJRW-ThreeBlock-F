# Hilbert of Jac(F)^J versus Hodge of a sextic fourfold

Author: Benjamin Stanley Frohman. © 2026. Apache-2.0.

## The numbers

Hilbert of the J-identity sector of Jac(F):

    (1, 426, 1751, 426, 1)    in degrees 0, 6, 12, 18, 24.
    dim = 2605.

Griffiths residue for a smooth sextic X = V(F) ⊂ P^5:

    (h^{4,0}, h^{3,1}, h^{2,2}_prim, h^{1,3}, h^{0,4}) = (1, 426, 1751, 426, 1).
    Full h^{2,2} = 1752 = 1751 + 1.
    b_4 = 2606 = 2605 + 1.

The extra 1 is ω² = h^2, the square of the hyperplane class. It is algebraic.

## What this closes

A consistency check. F is a degree-6 homogeneous polynomial in six variables.
If V(F) is smooth, Ehresmann plus Gauss–Manin say its Hodge numbers equal those of
any other smooth sextic fourfold. Griffiths (1968–69) identifies the graded pieces
of Jac(F) with primitive Hodge groups. Imposing J_F-invariance (degree 0 mod 6)
cuts Jac(F) down to dimension 2605, matching b_4 − 1.

Chiodo–Ruan LG/CY then says the state space of FJRW(F, ⟨J⟩) has the same
bigaded dimensions as H^•(V(F)). That is a dimension match, already predicted
for any Calabi–Yau hypersurface with the CY weight condition.

## What this does not close

- Not a new computation of h^{2,2}. 1752 for a sextic fourfold is standard.
- Not the FJRW quantum ring of (F, ⟨J⟩). Dimensions are not structure constants.
- Not Term A: ∀ fourfolds, every rational (2,2)-class is algebraic.
- Not Term B: one named γ ∈ Hdg^2(X) outside im(cl_X).
- On this host the extra algebraic classes already written are [Π] and [S] = h^2 − [Π].
  They live in the rank-2 span Q h^2 + Q[Π]. They do not fill the 1751-dimensional
  primitive piece.
